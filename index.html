About

I am a Ph.D. student in Computer Science at the University of Arizona, advised by Sazzadur Rahaman in SPRLAB. My research is on the security of AI agents: I build program-analysis techniques that scale to the skills, tools, and memory that LLM agents download and run, and I study how those ecosystems can be attacked.

My current work includes Asgard, a static analyzer that finds harmful cross-language data flows in agent skills, and a study of memory poisoning, prompt injection, and sandbox security in AI coding assistants.

Before my Ph.D., I completed my B.S. in Computer Science at LUMS (Lahore University of Management Sciences), where I worked on software debloating under the mentorship of Dr. Fareed Zaffar.

Research

Jan 2026 – Present

Asgard: Static Detection of Malicious Agentic Skills

AI assistants extend themselves with skills downloaded from public hubs, which mix natural-language instructions with code in many languages. Asgard lowers both into one language-independent IR spanning natural language and 8 programming languages, compacts it, and tracks harmful data flows across files, languages, and skills.

85% F1 vs. 69% for the strongest static baseline on AsgardBench, our 472-skill ground-truth benchmark. Feeding Asgard's IR (65% fewer tokens) to industry LLM scanners keeps their detections unchanged at ~30% lower cost. We are now scanning all 83,263 public skills on ClawHub.

Sep 2026 – Present

Memory Poisoning & Sandbox Security in AI Coding Agents

Studying how persistent memory and agent sandboxes in AI coding assistants can be abused, by reverse-engineering agent runtimes and building instrumentation for controlled prompt-injection experiments.

Feb 2024 – May 2025

Ground-Truth Evaluation of Code Debloating

Built manually debloated ground truth for 11 real-world programs and used it to evaluate 8 debloaters. Test-driven tools wrongly remove 71–73% of functionality-critical code on average (up to 94%), while IR-level tools keep 77% of unneeded functions. With collaborators at SRI International, Google, and the University of Arizona.
